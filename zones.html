The Ocean Zones

The ocean is divided into distinct vertical layers — each with unique conditions, pressures, temperatures, and inhabitants.

0 – 200m

☀️ Sunlight Zone

Epipelagic

The topmost layer, flooded with sunlight. This is where photosynthesis occurs, powering nearly all oceanic food webs. Temperatures range from 3°C to 30°C depending on latitude. The vast majority of ocean life — and all marine plants — live here.

  • Light: Full sunlight penetrates
  • Pressure: 1 – 20 atm
  • Temperature: 3°C – 30°C
  • Life: Coral reefs, sharks, dolphins, tuna, plankton
200 – 1,000m

🌑 Twilight Zone

Mesopelagic

Only faint blue light filters into this realm. Bioluminescence begins here — creatures light up to communicate, hunt, and evade predators. Many animals migrate vertically each day, rising at night to feed in shallower waters.

  • Light: Dim, fading to none
  • Pressure: 20 – 100 atm
  • Temperature: 4°C – 8°C
  • Life: Lanternfish, hatchetfish, squid, siphonophores
1,000 – 4,000m

🌊 Midnight Zone

Bathypelagic

Total, absolute darkness. No sunlight has ever reached here. The cold is near-freezing and pressure is enormous. Life slows down — yet strange creatures have adapted, many with enormous eyes, fang-like teeth, and bodies that absorb almost all light.

  • Light: None — absolute darkness
  • Pressure: 100 – 400 atm
  • Temperature: 2°C – 4°C
  • Life: Anglerfish, viperfish, giant squid, sperm whales
4,000 – 6,000m

🌌 Abyssal Zone

Abyssopelagic

The vast abyssal plains stretch across 60% of Earth's surface. They are covered in fine sediment — "marine snow" — drifting down from above. Hydrothermal vents here support entire ecosystems independent of the sun, powered by chemosynthesis.

  • Light: None
  • Pressure: 400 – 600 atm
  • Temperature: 0°C – 3°C
  • Life: Sea cucumbers, sea spiders, snailfish, vent tube worms
6,000 – 11,034m

💀 Hadal Zone

Hadalpelagic — The Trenches

Named after Hades, the underworld. Found only in ocean trenches — the deepest scars on Earth's crust. The Mariana Trench's Challenger Deep reaches 11,034m — deeper than Everest is tall. Pressure here is 1,000× atmospheric. And yet, life persists.

  • Light: None
  • Pressure: 600 – 1,100 atm
  • Temperature: 1°C – 4°C
  • Life: Amphipods, snailfish (deepest fish known), foraminifera
